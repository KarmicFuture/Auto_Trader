What the cart is doing

Empty Taco is a one-cart, one-owner hot dog stand in Tampa. Weekdays we plant at a lunch spot. Nights and weekends we chase foot traffic — Ybor Saturday Market, local breweries, Gasparilla, Guavaween, and whatever driveway you put on the booking form.

The name is a leftover. The license is Florida’s simplest mobile food category: a hot dog cart. The operating model is lean on purpose — used cart, hitch on a car we already own, commissary kitchen by the day, no second employee.

  1. 01 You send a place and a time. Office park, backyard, brewery lot, baby shower, Tuesday lunch.
  2. 02 We confirm dogs and headcount. You get a simple yes, a menu mix, and a window we can actually hit.
  3. 03 We hitch up and show up. Steam, condiments, chips, drinks. Cash, Cash App, or Venmo.

The humans

Tech people who just love a hot dog.

We come from keyboards, not culinary school. Empty Taco is the proof-of-concept: can you stand up a licensed, cash-flowing food business in Tampa for under $2,000, in about three weeks, with an AI system carrying the paperwork, the math, and the weekly schedule?

AI drafts the LLC details, the DBPR narrative, the commissary terms, the daily P&L, the social captions, and the reorder list. A human still files the forms, drives the cart, handles the cash, and cooks the dog. Florida law is not optional, and neither is a hot steamer.

The owner’s job compresses to the parts that actually need hands: hitch, setup, serve, pack out. If you book us, those hands are the ones that show up at your place.
